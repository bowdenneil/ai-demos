What is this portal?
This portal is a collection of interactive demos that show what large language models (LLMs) can do in real business scenarios. Each demo is a working application — you type something in, and an AI processes it live. No slides, no mockups — real AI calls in real time.

How to use it: Pick any demo card, click it, and follow the instructions on screen. Most demos take 30–60 seconds to run. The AI models are hosted on Tensorx, an EU-based AI provider. Your data stays in Europe and is not stored after processing.

Who is this for? Anyone who wants to see AI in action — no technical knowledge required. The descriptions below explain what each demo does in plain terms.

🛡️ AI-vs-AI Incident Timeline Incident Response

Walk through the HuggingFace July 2026 security incident phase-by-phase. See how an autonomous AI agent attacked, and how AI-assisted detection, forensic analysis, and remediation responded — including the guardrail asymmetry problem.

How it works:
  • The demo presents 6 phases of a real security incident (the HuggingFace July 2026 breach)
  • Each phase shows security telemetry — timestamps, events, severity levels, source systems
  • You click "Run AI Analysis" on each phase
  • The AI processes the raw log data and explains what happened, maps it to MITRE ATT&CK techniques, and identifies what the attacker was doing
  • Phases: Initial Access → Lateral Movement → AI Detection → Forensic Analysis → Containment → The Asymmetry Problem
Why it matters: This is a real incident that happened in July 2026. An autonomous AI agent attacked HuggingFace, and they used AI to detect and respond. The demo shows the full timeline — attack and defense — and ends with a critical lesson: commercial AI models blocked the defenders from analysing attack artifacts, while the attacker had no such restrictions. This "guardrail asymmetry" is a problem every organisation needs to plan for.

Real-time split-screen: watch an autonomous AI agent attack a mock AI platform while a defense agent detects, contains, and remediates. Three scenarios including the HuggingFace supply chain attack, AI-driven ransomware, and insider data exfiltration.

🔍 AI Forensic Log Analyzer Forensic Analysis

Feed a realistic 17,000-event attack log to an LLM and watch it reconstruct the attack in real-time: timeline, IOCs, compromised credentials, MITRE ATT&CK mapping, and remediation playbook. Toggle between commercial (guardrail-blocked) and open-weight models.

How it works:
  • The demo loads a realistic attack log (simulated events from a HuggingFace-style breach)
  • You choose your analysis model: Commercial API (e.g., GPT, Claude) or Open-Weight (GLM 5.2 on-premise)
  • If you pick Commercial: you see the requests get blocked by safety guardrails — the model refuses to process attack commands, C2 addresses, and credentials (exactly what happened to HuggingFace)
  • If you pick Open-Weight: the AI runs the full forensic analysis — reconstructs the attack timeline, extracts IOCs (IPs, domains, hashes), maps MITRE ATT&CK techniques, identifies compromised credentials, and generates a remediation playbook
Why it matters: This demo is the most important one for security teams. It demonstrates the "guardrail asymmetry problem": commercial AI models can't tell the difference between an attacker and a defender, so they block incident responders from analysing real attack artifacts. Meanwhile, attackers use unrestricted models. The lesson: you need a capable open-weight model on your own infrastructure, vetted and ready before an incident. This also keeps sensitive attack data and credentials from leaving your environment.

🚀 Agentic Employee Onboarding Agentic Workflow

Chat with an AI onboarding coordinator powered by DeepSeek. 6 specialist agents run real LLM→tool→result loops, calling simulated IT systems (email, Slack, GitHub, inventory, contracts, VPN). Watch their reasoning live. Inject failures to test resilience. Onboarded employees are saved to a database.

How it works:
  • The AI coordinator (DeepSeek) greets you and asks for the new hire's details naturally — name, email, role, department, start date, location, manager
  • You can provide info one piece at a time or all at once — the AI adapts
  • You can use voice input (mic button) or type — voice auto-submits
  • Once collected, 6 specialist agents spin up: Identity & Access, Hardware & Assets, Documentation & Compliance, Knowledge & Training, Workspace Setup, and Orchestrator
  • Each agent runs a real agentic loop — the LLM decides which tool to call, reads the result, adapts, and decides the next step (not a scripted checklist)
  • Agents call 10 simulated IT system APIs: email provisioning, Slack invites, GitHub org access, hardware inventory, employment contracts, e-signature approvals, VPN configuration, compliance training, calendar scheduling, and asset registration
  • Agents share state with each other — Identity creates the email, Workspace uses it to configure VPN; Hardware assigns an asset tag, Orchestrator logs it in the final report
  • Some agents wait for others to finish (dependencies): Knowledge and Workspace wait for Identity; Orchestrator waits for all 5 others
  • Each agent panel shows a live reasoning log with timestamps — thinking (🤔), tool calls (🔧), results (📋), successes (✅), and warnings (⚠️)
  • Inject failures mid-workflow with the buttons at the top: laptop out of stock, email collision, approval delayed, VPN down — agents detect the failure and adapt (generate email variant, defer to Day 2, flag for procurement)
  • The Orchestrator validates all tasks, detects issues (e.g. duplicate training), and generates a personalized welcome packet with a first-week schedule
  • The employee is saved to a persistent database — type "review employees" or "list employees" in the chat to see everyone who's been onboarded
Why it matters: This demo shows the difference between a chatbot and an AI agent. A chatbot answers questions. An agent decides what to do, calls tools to do it, reads the results, adapts to failures, and coordinates with other agents — exactly like a human IT team would, but at machine speed. The simulated IT systems are realistic (inventory depletes, emails collide, approvals get delayed) so you can see how the agents handle real-world messiness. The failure injection feature lets you test resilience live — what happens when the laptop is out of stock or the VPN is down? The agent doesn't crash, it adapts.
